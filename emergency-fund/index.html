Let’s start with Trials

The Emergency Fund faced a challenge: users found it difficult to subscribe immediately due to the high cost and the 14-day activation period. To improve feature acceptance and user conversion, we introduced 7-day trials and applied them across several new acquisition funnels.

EF Trial Immediately After Care Subscription

Another company service, Petcube Care, has a larger user base and a much higher payment conversion rate. We decided to leverage this by encouraging this group of users to subscribe to the Emergency Fund (EF).

To do this, we offered a trial subscription to EF immediately after a user subscribed to Petcube Care. Since it’s easier to sign up for something free right after making a purchase, we framed it as a gift for subscribing.

As a result, this became one of the most popular points for EF trial sign-ups.

Reward for User Feedback on Camera Stream Quality

Another conversion point for EF was the NPS screens. We gather user feedback on video streaming quality, which is generally positive. However, there were no rewards for users in this flow. So, we thought we could reward them with a free trial period.

We incorporated a few psychological tricks into this flow:

  1. Show users what their trial will look like and remind them when it ends.

  2. Highlight that their journey has already started, with the first step completed.

  3. Create curiosity by hiding what awaits the user, prompting them to swipe up.

Reward for successful Care onboarding completion

Petcube Care has a specific onboarding process, with success and stages tracked by a tracker. Our team came up with the idea to reward users with a free EF trial period for completing this onboarding process.

Overall, these implementations led to an increase in the user base and improved payment conversion.

New Onboarding Screens

Users were unclear about the terms of the service. How to receive the $3,000 for pet treatment, where this amount would be sent, how many pets the service covers, and the waiting period. Previously, this information was detailed on the website and paywall screens, but users often overlooked it. To address this, we decided to present the terms more engagingly and clearly.

We created an onboarding experience using a familiar UI pattern — stories. These stories used simple animations to explain all the terms. We even decided to change the style, to make a pattern break for user and animate it easily. This led to fewer support inquiries about EF terms and features, better user understanding, and fewer cancellations due to unmet expectations.
