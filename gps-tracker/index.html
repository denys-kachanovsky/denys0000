New hardware product launch

At Petcube, I was also involved in designing a new product— the Petcube GPS Tracker. Just imagine: you attach this tracker to your dog's collar and can track their location, monitor their walk history, the distance they cover, and the calories they burn.

When I joined the design phase of this part of the app, a substantial amount of work had already been done by other designers—practically everything, to be honest. After everything was developed, the project stakeholders decided to conduct user testing, following which we made improvements to the initial version. In this case study, I'll share how this process unfolded.

User testing

The first version of the app was created based on hypotheses from the design and product teams, as well as an analysis of competitive products. To validate these hypotheses, the company conducted user testing involving around 20 participants. They were tasked with setting up the tracker in the app and using its various features.

After conducting the research, we gathered many valuable insights and identified several critical issues:

  1. Users didn’t understand what a Wi-Fi zone was and why they needed to create one.

  2. They were also confused about what live location was and how it differed from regular tracking mode. They weren't sure if tracking worked without enabling live location.

  3. It was unclear how to view the walk history and visited places.

  4. There were also a few problems with the setup process.

Device Setup

We improved the communication during the device setup process through animations and enhanced texts. During this process, we also discovered that the tracker might discharge during shipping, meaning users could receive it with a low battery. To address this, we communicated this information on one of the screens in the setup flow.

Power saving mode

The Wi-Fi zone is a designated area where the tracker can connect to Wi-Fi. This feature helps the tracker conserve battery life and transmit location data more frequently. According to our survey, users didn’t understand the value of this feature.

To improve clarity, we enhanced our communication and renamed this function to "Power Saving Mode."

Location History

Location History is a feature in the app where users can view their dog's walk history over the past few days. We moved this feature to a dedicated screen and added convenient tools for tracking the dog's location over time.

Additionally, users can utilize the heatmap view to see the most frequently visited places within a given time frame.

Improvement after user feedback

After releasing this feature, we received positive feedback from users. However, we wanted to continue improving it, so we decided to add the ability to highlight individual walks and view them separately.

Virtual Fences

Virtual Fences is a feature that allows users to set boundaries on a map. For example, you can create a Safe Zone around your yard and receive notifications if your dog leaves that area. Similarly, you can set up a No-Go Zone for places where your dog isn’t allowed to go.

To enhance this feature, we designed an intuitive control that communicates the radius of each zone. Additionally, users can name the zones, making it clear where the dog is when a notification is received.

It was a valuable experience in UX design and working with hardware, as well as in analyzing and responding to user feedback. I want to extend my gratitude to Andriy Klen for this opportunity, and to Oleh Mayhopar and Oleh Smieltsov—exceptional designers and professionals. Our collaborative working and brainstorming sessions were truly amazing.
